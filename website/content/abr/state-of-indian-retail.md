Quick commerce arrived in Indian cities with a promise that was easy to
understand and hard to price: everything, in ten minutes. Three years on, the
consumer habit has clearly stuck. The question that remains open is whether the
economics have.

## Where the money goes

The cost stack is dominated by three lines — rider payouts, dark-store rent and
discounting. Each behaves differently as a city matures, and averaging them
across markets hides more than it reveals.

## What to watch

- Order density per dark store, not order growth
- The share of basket value from high-margin categories
- Whether discounting falls when a market reaches duopoly

## The argument

Profitability in quick commerce is a density problem before it is a pricing
problem. Operators who solve for orders per store per hour reach sustainable
contribution margins well before those who chase headline GMV.
